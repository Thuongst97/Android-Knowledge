The 5-Layer AAOS 14 Architecture

Every feature in the car — from the A/C button to the speedometer — flows through the same five-layer pipeline. Understand this pipeline and you understand 80% of AAOS.

2.1High-level architecture diagrams

Three complementary views of the same system. Start with the system overview (what exists), then zoom into CarService (where vehicle policy lives), then the partitions & processes view (who ships what, and where the process boundaries are). Hover a component to trace its direct connections (everything else dims) and read a one-line description.

AAOS keeps the entire standard Android stack and adds the car-specific pieces: the Car API, CarService, car native daemons and automotive HALs. Every crossing of the amber Treble line is a Stable AIDL interface.

ICarImpl creates and owns every car service. HAL-facing services never talk to the VHAL directly: they go through VehicleHal, which multiplexes one IVehicle connection into per-domain “HAL services”.

The amber line is the Treble boundary: only stable, versioned interfaces (AIDL with @VintfStability, sysprops, RRO-able resources) may cross it. This is what lets Google/OEMs update /system independently of /vendor.

Analogy: an international conference

Apps (L1) are delegates speaking English. The Car API (L2) is the standard request-to-speak form; CarService is the secretariat checking delegate badges. The MCU & bus (L5) are the foreign delegations who only understand their own language.

2.5Treble boundary & VINTF

Since Android 8 (Project Treble), Google's framework (/system) and OEM/SoC-vendor hardware code (/vendor) are separated by stable, versioned interfaces. For AAOS the most important one is android.hardware.automotive.vehicle.IVehicle.

HIDL → AIDL

Android 8–12 used VHAL HIDL @2.0. Since Android 13 the VHAL uses Stable AIDL (@VintfStability). Android 14 ships VHAL AIDL V2 and still supports HIDL VHALs through a compatibility client in CarService.

VINTF manifest

The vendor declares “I provide IVehicle/default version 2”; the framework compatibility matrix declares “I require IVehicle”. A mismatch fails the build or the boot — catching problems early.

✅
Practical consequence

An OEM can upgrade Android 14 → 15 on /system while keeping the VHAL unchanged on /vendor, as long as the interface remains compatible. That is why apps or CarService must never call vendor libraries directly.

2.6The Vehicle Property model

All App ⇄ VHAL communication boils down to a single concept: the Vehicle Property. Each property has a 32-bit ID that encodes its own meaning:

propId = group[31:28] | areaType[27:24] | valueType[23:16] | id[15:0]

Analogy

A property ID is like a postal code that also encodes the parcel type: just from the number you know the region (group), district (area type), whether it is a letter or a parcel (value type) and the street number (id). The area ID is the specific room in that house — driver's seat or passenger's seat.
