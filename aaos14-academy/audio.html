Multi-Zone Audio

In a car, the driver hears navigation prompts, the front passenger is on a call and the kids in the back watch a movie on headphones — all at once. AAOS solves this with audio zones, volume groups, per-zone audio focus and context-based routing.

⏱ 20 min read🎯 Intermediate📂 service/src/com/android/car/audio

3.2.1Core concepts

On a phone, audio follows “stream types” (STREAM_MUSIC, STREAM_RING…) and almost everything comes out of the same speaker. AAOS replaces this with a hierarchy:

AudioAttributes.usage → Audio Context → Volume Group → Output Device (bus) ∈ Audio Zone

Audio Context

A semantic category derived from usage: MUSIC, NAVIGATION, VOICE_COMMAND, CALL_RING, CALL, ALARM, NOTIFICATION, SYSTEM_SOUND, EMERGENCY, SAFETY, VEHICLE_STATUS, ANNOUNCEMENT. Apps never choose speakers — they only declare what the sound is.

Output Device (bus)

A logical output with an address such as bus0_media_out. The Audio HAL delivers PCM to the DSP/amplifier; mixing and distribution to physical speakers usually happens in a DSP outside Android.

Volume Group

Contexts that share a single volume slider. E.g. the “Media” group contains MUSIC + ANNOUNCEMENT. Gain can be applied in Android (software) or sent to the DSP via the Audio Control HAL.

Audio Zone

A set of devices serving one area of the cabin, tied to an occupant zone. Each zone has its own volume groups and its own focus stack. Zone 0 is always the primary zone.

🎭
Analogy

Think of a restaurant with several private dining rooms (audio zones). Each room has its own speakers and volume knob (volume groups). A coordinator (CarAudioService) decides which track plays in which room, and an urgent announcement (EMERGENCY) may interrupt. Diners (apps) just say “I'd like background music”; they never rewire the speakers themselves.

Real deployments

This example uses four zones for four seats to illustrate the idea. Many production cars have only a primary zone for all cabin speakers, with secondary zones for the RSE headphone jacks/Bluetooth headsets. The number of zones and their seat mapping are entirely OEM-configured.

3.2.3Audio focus policy

When an app calls AudioManager.requestAudioFocus(), the zone's CarAudioFocus looks up an interaction matrix between the context currently holding focus and the context requesting it. There are three outcomes:

EXCLUSIVE

The requester gets focus; the holder loses it (transiently or permanently). Example: an incoming call while music plays.

CONCURRENT

Both play; the holder is usually ducked (attenuated). Example: a navigation prompt over music.

REJECT

The request is denied (AUDIOFOCUS_REQUEST_FAILED). Example: a music app requesting focus during a call.

3.2.4Routing & XML configuration

The routing path when a media app plays music on a rear-seat screen:

  1. The app creates an AudioTrack/MediaPlayer with AudioAttributes.USAGE_MEDIA. The app runs as the Android user of that zone (MUMD) or its UID is mapped to the zone.
  2. CarAudioService has registered a dynamic audio policy with the AudioPolicyManager: each (zone, context) pair maps to a bus device.
  3. AudioFlinger mixes and writes PCM to the Audio HAL device bus200_audio_zone_2.
  4. The OEM's DSP/amplifier delivers the signal to the rear-left headphones. Ducking/fading can be signalled down through the IAudioControl HAL.
🆕
New in Android 14

The configuration file moves to version 3: each zone can have several zoneConfigs and switch between them at runtime (e.g. a rear zone switching between cabin speakers and Bluetooth headphones), “core audio routing” based on framework audio attributes is supported, and per-zone volume groups and muting are extended.

⚠️
Mandatory rules

Every zone must map all audio contexts; each bus device belongs to exactly one zone; bus addresses must match the Audio HAL's audio_policy_configuration.xml. Violations make CarAudioService fail at startup.
