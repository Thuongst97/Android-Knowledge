Foundational Concepts & Ecosystem

Before touching code, answer three questions: what “Android in the car” actually means, how it differs from a phone, and what hardware it runs on inside a vehicle.

⏱ 15 min read🎯 Beginner📦 AOSP android-14.0.0

1.1Android Automotive OS at a glance

Android Automotive OS (AAOS) is a full Android platform that runs natively on the car's own computer. Apps are installed on the car, so no phone is needed. Android also becomes part of the vehicle: it can read speed and gear and control climate, seats and power. It does this through two additions to normal Android: CarService, which decides who may do what, and the Vehicle HAL, which translates requests into the car's own bus signals.

What AAOS adds to Android

🎛️

Vehicle properties

Read and set car state through CarPropertyManager → CarService → VHAL.

 🔋

Vehicle power cycle

Follows the ignition: Garage Mode, Suspend-to-RAM and Hibernation with the MCU.

 🔊

Audio zones

Separate mixes, volume groups and audio focus for each seat zone.

 🖥️

Multiple displays

Cluster, center, passenger and rear screens mapped to occupant zones.

 👥

Concurrent users

A headless system user plus one Android user per occupant (MUMD).
🚦

Driving restrictions

CarUxRestrictionsManager limits what the UI shows while the car is moving.

🔐

Vehicle security

Car permissions, SELinux and hardware isolation keep apps off the buses.

 🧩

OEM customization

RROs, plug-in services and OemCarService extend AAOS without forking it.
🎭
Analogy

AAOS is the built-in computer of a smart building. It runs the screens and apps people use, and it also controls the lights, blinds and air conditioning. Visitors never touch the plant room: every request goes through the facilities desk (CarService), which checks their badge. The desk then hands the job to the technician who speaks the machines' language (the VHAL).

ℹ️
Note

AAOS is not Android Auto. Android Auto projects a phone's apps onto the car screen, while AAOS is the car's operating system — and it can still offer Android Auto or CarPlay projection as an app. With GAS requires a Google license (e.g. Volvo, Polestar, GM); without GAS the OEM builds its own store and services, common among Chinese OEMs.

1.3Real-world deployment architecture

Android does not talk to the engine or the A/C directly. A real car contains two computing worlds: a high-performance SoC running Android (not real-time), and a real-time MCU guarding the vehicle buses. Click any block to see its role.

Analogy

The SoC is a brilliant, multitasking CEO who is sometimes stuck in meetings (GC pauses, CPU load). The MCU is the gatekeeper who never leaves their post: first awake when the car is unlocked, responding in microseconds, and deciding when to wake the CEO or send them to sleep. Safety functions must never depend on whether the CEO is free.

Why not wire CAN straight into Android?

  • Real time: Linux/Android cannot guarantee hard deadlines; an MCU services CAN signals on steady 10–100 ms cycles.
  • Boot time: Android takes 10–30 s to boot; the rear-view camera and cluster must appear within ~2 s by regulation → handled by the MCU/RTOS.
  • Power: the MCU is always powered from the battery, wakes the SoC on unlock and cuts SoC power for sleep.
  • Security: a gateway filters frames, so a compromised Android cannot inject forged brake or steering commands.

1.4Key takeaways

AAOS = Android + the vehicle

Full Android plus the Car framework (CarService, Car API) and automotive HALs (VHAL, Audio Control, EVS).

Android Auto ≠ AAOS

Auto is projection from a phone; AAOS is the operating system running on the car.

Two computing worlds

SoC (Android, feature-rich) ⇄ MCU (real-time, vehicle bus). The VHAL is the bridge between them.

Automotive specifics

Multiple displays and users, vehicle-state power management, driving UX restrictions, safety and security requirements.
